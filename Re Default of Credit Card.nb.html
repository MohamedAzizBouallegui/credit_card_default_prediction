Title of the Subject

Analysis of Credit Card Transactions for Early Detection of Defaults.

Subject Description

This study focuses on the in-depth analysis of credit card holders’ behavior with the aim of early detection of potential signs of payment default. By examining spending habits, repayment patterns, and other financial behaviors, the objective is to identify predictive indicators that enable the assessment of default risk. The goal of this research is to facilitate a proactive approach in managing payment defaults, thereby offering financial institutions more accurate and faster solutions to evaluate and mitigate the inherent risks associated with credit card transactions. Work to be Done

• Data preprocessing (Outliers, missing values, data quality analysis) • Exploratory data analysis (Univariate analysis, bivariate analysis, Data visualization, …) • Variable transformation (Normalization, discretization, …) • Correlation analysis, analysis of variance, independence tests • Dashboard with RShiny.

Information About the Dataset:

The dataset used is the “Default of Credit Card Clients Dataset” from UCI ML on Kaggle. This dataset contains information on payment defaults, demographic factors, credit data, payment history, and billing statements of credit card clients in Taiwan from April 2005 to September 2005.

# Function to detect outliers using IQR
detect_outliers_iqr <- function(x) {
  Q1 <- quantile(x, 0.25, na.rm = TRUE)
  Q3 <- quantile(x, 0.75, na.rm = TRUE)
  IQR <- Q3 - Q1
  lower_bound <- Q1 - 1.5 * IQR
  upper_bound <- Q3 + 1.5 * IQR
  return(x < lower_bound | x > upper_bound)
}

There are several possible reasons: •Higher-income or financially stronger customers may qualify for much larger credit limits. •Customers with a long history of responsible repayment may gradually receive higher limits. •Businesses, premium customers, or high-value customers may naturally receive much larger limits than ordinary customers. •Customers who consistently spend a lot may be given higher limits to accommodate their usage. •Lenders may assign limits based on perceived probability of default. Low-risk customers can receive substantially higher limits. •Long-standing customers may have earned increased limits over time. •A few extremely high values could represent corporate accounts, negotiated limits, or exceptional approvals. •The large values could also be data-entry errors or genuine statistical outliers.
